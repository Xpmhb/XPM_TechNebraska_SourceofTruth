01  /  CORE STRATEGY

Executive Strategy

The Summit is the content harvest; the six-month program is the editorial system.




|  | Core strategic ideaUse the 2026 Tech Nebraska Summit to create a deep reserve of people, ideas, photography, interviews, and session content—then deliberately taper Summit material into a year-round statewide content program rather than publishing an event recap dump. |

| --- | --- |




What this program is trying to accomplish

• Grow membership—especially by creating a stronger pathway from valuable content to email subscription and, ultimately, membership.

• Build credible thought leadership around technology, business, workforce, policy, and Nebraska competitiveness.

• Expand statewide awareness by consistently showing the people, businesses, resources, and ideas operating across Nebraska.

• Create content that is engaging and human without sacrificing executive-level credibility.

• Turn Tech Nebraska’s board, advisory network, members, founders, and partners into an active content and amplification network.

North-star editorial stance




|  | The Nebraska builder is the hero.The main character is the Nebraska member, individual, company, founder, operator, student, or practitioner. Tech Nebraska is the guide: it connects, advocates, interprets, convenes, and amplifies. |

| --- | --- |




What success should feel like by April




| Priority | End-state |

| --- | --- |

| 1 | Members, board leaders, and ecosystem partners regularly participate in and amplify Tech Nebraska content. |

| 2 | Tech Nebraska has built a recognizable statewide content property—not simply an event social feed. |

| 3 | XPM and Tech Nebraska know which topics, people, formats, regions, and CTAs perform best and can build the next strategy from evidence. |

| 4 | Membership growth is measurable and the content-to-subscribe-to-membership path is clearer. |




02  /  CORE STRATEGY

Editorial Positioning & Voice

A practitioner-led statewide voice, not another technology news outlet.

Working differentiation hypothesis




|  | Editorial whitespaceTech Nebraska should not try to out-report technology media. Its advantage is access to Nebraska’s practitioners, enterprise leaders, founders, policymakers, members, and regional ecosystem. The content should answer: What is happening? Why does it matter here? What are people actually learning? How can someone participate? |

| --- | --- |




Brand foundations already present in the source of truth




| Brand principle | Editorial implication |

| --- | --- |

| Connecting People | Feature the collisions, introductions, peer networks, and organizations that make the ecosystem useful. |

| Advancing Technology | Prioritize practical deployment, modernization, AI, cyber, infrastructure, and measurable business use. |

| Elevating the Good Life | Show that high-quality technology careers, companies, and innovation can be built across Nebraska. |

| Convene → Advocate → Elevate | Use this as a membership-value lens: access, influence, and amplification—not a generic “join us” pitch. |




Tone mix




| Mode | Share | How it should feel |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Substantive | ~70% | Credible, useful, specific, business-oriented, nonpartisan. |

| Human / engaging | ~20% | Real people, candid clips, quick questions, behind-the-scenes energy. |

| Experimental | ~10% | Playful formats, rapid interviews, AI-assisted creative, new storytelling structures—without losing credibility. |







|  | Creative ruleReal photography and video should lead. Reusable design systems and AI-assisted graphics support the story; they should not make the feed feel synthetic or over-templated. |

03  /  CORE STRATEGY

Content Architecture

Five jobs, one weekly operating rhythm, one flexible slot.

Five recurring content jobs




| Content job | Purpose | Typical sources |

| --- | --- | --- |

| 1. Member + Builder Stories | Show who is building, operating, leading, hiring, investing, and creating across Nebraska. | Member interviews, ticket-holder audit, Summit interviews, company assets, member social content. |

| 2. Technology in Practice / AI | Make technology useful and concrete; translate hype into real business application. | Panels, CIO/founder interviews, member use cases, current AI/tech developments. |

| 3. Nebraska Tech Intelligence | Explain policy, workforce, capital, economic development, and technology changes that matter to Nebraska. | Emily/Shelley priorities, board expertise, policy updates, Summit sessions, public developments. |

| 4. Ecosystem + Opportunity | Help people discover resources, organizations, programs, jobs, grants, accelerators, events, and pathways. | Member network, community organizations, universities, regional ecosystem. |

| 5. Tech Nebraska / Membership | Make participation value visible through stories—not promotional claims. | Board/member perspectives, programs, impact, events, advocacy, connections. |




Normal weekly rhythm




| Slot | Default job | Notes |

| --- | --- | --- |

| 1 | Member / Builder / Nebraska Story | Anchor the week in a real person, company, or regional story. |

| 2 | Tech in Practice / AI | Practical insight, use case, speaker clip, or “what this means for Nebraska.” |

| 3 | Intelligence / Ecosystem | Policy, workforce, funding, resource, opportunity, or useful current development. |

| 4 | Flex | Timely priority, membership story, event push, high-performing series, or stakeholder request. |







|  | Cadence guardrailPlan roughly 14–16 unique social pieces per month, including 6–8 short-form videos. Use minimums by category rather than forcing an identical weekly formula when the story quality is not there. |

04  /  CORE STRATEGY

Distribution, Geography & Participation

LinkedIn-first distribution with intentional statewide representation.

Platform hierarchy




| Platform | Role |

| --- | --- |

| LinkedIn | Primary editorial platform for members, executives, founders, board voices, thought leadership, practical insight, and amplification. |

| Facebook | Community distribution, events, photos, broader civic/ecosystem storytelling, and adapted thought leadership. |

| Instagram | Human/visual discovery, short-form video, photography, lighter personality, and young-professional/student reach. |




Regional operating model

• Maintain a weekly regional intention: every week deliberately source at least one story from a target part of Nebraska.

• Maintain quarterly accountability: track which regions have and have not been represented, then actively source gaps.

• Use community-college regions as the likely geographic foundation, but finalize the exact taxonomy with Emily and Shelley before hard-coding it.

• Do not publish a weak story simply to hit geography; use the scorecard to improve sourcing rather than lower the editorial bar.

• Start with existing Northeast Nebraska microsummit material as an obvious low-cost proof point once the regional map is approved.

Flagship discovery series




|  | Working concept: “Built Here” / statewide discovery seriesCreate a recognizable recurring format that helps people discover Nebraska companies, builders, technologies, and careers they did not know existed. The final name and positioning should be ideated with the Tech Nebraska team. |

Candidate story structure

• What are you building?

• Why does it matter?

• Why Nebraska / what does Nebraska need next?

05  /  CORE STRATEGY

Six-Month Editorial Arc

Taper Summit content while progressively establishing a year-round Nebraska technology voice.




| Period | Editorial role | Summit share / emphasis |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Oct. 22–31 | Summit launch burst | Separate from the monthly cadence: thank-you, recap/sizzle, photos, early clips, priority amplification. |

| November | Summit Afterglow + series introduction | ~75% Summit / ~25% new evergreen. Use the strongest people and ideas while beginning the recurring system. |

| December | Transition to Nebraska people + technology | Balanced mix of builder stories, practical tech/AI, ecosystem resources, and remaining Summit assets. |

| January | Reintroduce Tech Nebraska without making Tech Nebraska the hero | Explain who Tech Nebraska serves, what it connects, what it advocates for, and how people can participate—through member-led stories. |

| February | Statewide builders, careers + practical technology | Strengthen regional rotation, student/early-career opportunity, member use cases, and recurring discovery formats. |

| March | Policy, competitiveness + ecosystem | 2–3 policy/economic pieces in a normal month, flexing upward when legislative activity warrants. |

| April | Proof, participation + renewal bridge | Show member/board participation, strongest recurring series, statewide coverage, learning, and measurable momentum. |







|  | Important distinctionThe six-month program is the managed publishing and optimization period. The Summit content library should outlive the retainer and can continue fueling Tech Nebraska well beyond April. |

06  /  CORE STRATEGY

Summit Content Engine

Capture enough source material to create a six-month reserve without overworking the crew.

Realistic interview capacity




| Capture type | Target | Planning note |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Scheduled substantive interviews | 12–15 across Oct. 20–21 | Use 15-minute blocks even when the interview itself is 8–10 minutes. Protect transitions and lunch. |

| Quick-hit / opportunistic interviews | 6–10 | 60–120 second responses; use for compilations, quick opinions, attendee energy, and discovery. |

| Priority order | Emily + attending governing board + 3 priority stakeholders + selected builders/ecosystem leaders | Use the VIP reception aggressively to reduce Summit-day pressure. |




Session-derived content potential




| Source | Estimated output |

| --- | --- |

| ~13 program/session units | Potential long-form archive/source recordings. |

| ~8 major panels/discussions | Target 4–5 candidate short clips per strong session, not a forced quota. |

| Keynotes / opening / closing / lightning | Additional short-form insights and thematic compilations. |

| Session recording bank | Rough planning ceiling: ~45–60 candidate session shorts, subject to editorial quality. |




Interview-derived content potential

• A strong 10-minute interview can yield 2–4 distinct pieces: profile, practical technology insight, Nebraska/ecosystem perspective, and quote/photo content.

• 12–15 structured interviews could create roughly 25–40 candidate interview/profile pieces.

• Quick-hit responses can support compilation formats such as “What should Nebraska build next?” rather than 10 isolated testimonials.

| Ecosystem proof | Founders + enterprise leaders, board + attendees, corporate + startup, policy + industry, younger professionals, regional representation. |

| Details | Badges, programs, sponsor activation, hands/devices, products, room texture, technology moments, branded environmental shots. |







|  | Photo targetPlan for roughly 150–250 genuinely usable edited selects—not an indiscriminate event-photo dump. |

09  /  CORE STRATEGY

Website, Blog & Email Integration

One master editorial system; channel responsibilities remain distinct.

Website role

• Hybrid content hub: deep stories, resources, videos, and evergreen content should live on the Tech Nebraska website when useful; lightweight timely posts can remain social-only.

• XPM manages routine website content publishing, page updates, on-page SEO, metadata, internal linking, and priority keyword improvements under the engagement.

• Major redesigns, custom development, and significant technical SEO remain outside the monthly base scope unless separately approved.

• Whether every Summit session receives a dedicated video/archive page remains a stakeholder decision with Emily and Shelley.

Blog / article strategy




|  | Existing editorial strengthThe current blog has already evolved toward substantive practitioner-driven content around applied AI, founders, capital, industry modernization, and policy. The opportunity is not to invent a new voice from scratch—it is to make the strongest version of that voice consistent. |

| --- | --- |




• Scope allows up to two articles/month, but the exact format mix (deep features vs. shorter timely/SEO pieces) should be decided in the strategy session.

• People-focused articles are a likely high-value lane, but they should reveal useful lessons, tensions, and Nebraska context rather than read as vanity profiles.

• SEO/search opportunity should help choose between otherwise equal stories, not override editorial usefulness.

Email role

• XPM does not own the newsletter send or email program.

• The master editorial calendar should deliberately feed strong stories, clips, articles, opportunities, and membership narratives into Shelley’s email workflow.

• Working conversion hypothesis: valuable content → subscribe → nurture → membership. Validate the actual membership journey with Emily before optimizing around it.

11  /  CORE STRATEGY

Measurement & Learning Loop

Measure action and ecosystem participation before vanity reach.

Ongoing KPI priority




| Rank | KPI | What it tells us |

| --- | --- | --- |

| 1 | Membership growth | Is content moving people toward deeper participation? |

| 2 | Engagement quality | Are people commenting, sharing, saving, discussing, and responding—not merely viewing? |

| 3 | Member / partner amplification | Are featured organizations, board members, speakers, and partners redistributing the content? |

| 4 | Geographic representation | Are we actually telling a statewide story rather than defaulting to Omaha/Lincoln? |

| 5 | Audience growth | Is the addressable community around Tech Nebraska expanding? |




April renewal / proof hierarchy

• First: member, board, and ecosystem participation/amplification is visibly stronger.

• Second: Tech Nebraska has a recognizable statewide editorial/content property.

• Third: the team has a data-backed next strategy based on six months of performance.

• Fourth: membership growth is measurable and the conversion path is clearer.

Learning loop




|  | Create → Publish → Measure → Learn → AdjustEach monthly review should identify which topics, people, regions, hooks, formats, platforms, and CTAs deserve more or less future attention. The objective is not to defend the original calendar; it is to improve it. |

Questions the strategy session should answer

• What does membership growth actually look like today—from first touch through joining?

• Which board/advisory members does Tech Nebraska most want to activate, and around which topics?

• Which Nebraska regions are most underrepresented or strategically important over the next six months?

• Which policy/advocacy topics are approved priorities during the engagement?

• What existing content, events, deadlines, programs, and member communications need to be reflected in the six-month calendar?

• Which article/session formats have historically generated the most readership, discussion, and stakeholder value?
